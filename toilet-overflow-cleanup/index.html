Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in New Britain

An overflowing toilet seems like something towels can handle, except the water keeps moving past the tile. The gap at the drain pipe, the wall and the space under the baseboard all take water in. The contents of that water matter more than anything. Water from the tank or supply line is clean. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

The first thing our New Britain team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. If it is clean, we extract it and dry the area. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. Then we check the subfloor, the wall bases and the room underneath with moisture meters so nothing is left wet.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the WaterBehind the toilet is a small valve, and turning it fully clockwise shuts the water off. A stuck valve can be worked around by taking off the tank lid and lifting the float, which stops the fill.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. A blockage in the drain line means water sent down any fixture can back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterBowl water, or any water with waste in it, means kids and pets stay out of the room. Should you have to cross it, wear gloves and waterproof footwear.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathFor an upstairs toilet, look at the ceiling underneath. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Contain Clean WaterOld towels can stop clean water before it gets to a hallway or carpet. Anything contaminated is a job for people with proper gear and disinfecting products.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Supply lines that give out or tanks that crack, releasing clean water across the floor.
  • Tank parts, the fill valve or the flapper, that jam and let water run nonstop.
  • Blocked traps and drain lines, which leave the bowl nowhere to empty but onto the floor.
  • Waste rising out of the lowest toilet in the house because the main sewer line is blocked.
  • Water escaping at the base with every flush because the wax ring has worn out.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

Sitting at floor level, door casings and baseboards wick water up from their lower edge. Fiberboard trim, the most common kind, swells and splits and never returns to shape.

Odor That Returns

Contaminated water that dries inside a wall base or under flooring leaves a smell that returns in humid weather until the source material is taken out.

Bacterial Contamination

Water from the drain or bowl deposits bacteria in drywall, baseboards, carpet and pad. Anything porous is normally removed, since it cannot be disinfected with confidence.

Ceiling Below

Water from an upstairs bathroom runs down the drain pipe and sits on the drywall ceiling underneath. Stains and sagging follow, and now and then the ceiling comes down.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Many homeowners policies cover a toilet overflow when it is sudden and accidental, such as a failed fill valve or an unexpected clog. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. If the toilet leaked at its base for months, that damage is usually not covered. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • An upstairs location, where water can reach the walls or ceiling of the floor below.
  • Whether the water was contaminated, because those overflows take more protection, disinfection and labor.
  • The distance the water spread, for example to a closet, a hallway or carpet in a bedroom.
  • Any need to remove the vanity or toilet so the area under it can be cleaned and dried.
