Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What New Britain Homeowners Should Know

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

What It Looks Like

  • A musty smell that is stronger near one wall or outlet
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
  • New gaps along casings and baseboard tops as swollen material shifts
  • Baseboards that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • A wall surface that gives, feels mushy or breaks apart under your thumb
What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold Inside Cavities

Paper lining, darkness and stagnant air make the hidden side of a wall a perfect place for mold. You can have a clean looking painted surface with heavy growth right behind it.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Outlets and wiring are mounted near the floor, exactly where the water gathers. Until that circuit is inspected, avoid plugging anything into the affected wall.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Water in a perimeter wall may reach the sheathing on the far side of the cavity. It gets a moisture reading prior to closing, since it holds dampness a long time.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

What We Do

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Wicking Begins

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

It is a level, straight line cut in the drywall 12 to 24 inches higher than the top wet reading. Everything below the line is removed so the insulation can come out and the studs can dry. Cutting in a clean line makes the new drywall easier to install and the finished seam easier to hide.
Blending the repair is part of the job, and it usually disappears. We tape and finish the seam, match the wall texture and reinstall or replace the baseboard. Paint is the hard part, since older paint fades and a patch can show. Painting corner to corner across the entire wall typically solves it.
